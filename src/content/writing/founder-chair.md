I left ExxonMobil in 2021 after 19 years. I didn't leave because anything was wrong. I left because I wanted to build the thing I couldn't buy as a customer.

That's a sentence I've said in a few pitches. It's true, but it's not the whole story.

## What nineteen years actually teaches you

The first thing the trading floor teaches you is that the price is always there. You can see it, update it, reason about it. Markets are transparent — at least in the sense that everyone with a screen can see the same number.

The second thing it teaches you is that the price is never the whole story. The spread, the basis, the forward curve — these are the things that actually matter. The spot price is the headline. The structure underneath is where the money is.

The third thing — and this takes most of the nineteen years to really absorb — is that the physical world doesn't care about your model. A vessel is delayed. A pipeline is full. A refinery runs at reduced rate because of a maintenance window in January that was planned in the previous April. The market price for a product is not the same as the value of that product to a specific physical operation at a specific moment.

> The structure underneath the trade is the trade.

## What Kanay is built to solve

When I started Kanay, I kept coming back to one question: why does the energy market lack the structure that would let a good trading desk operate at scale?

The answer I landed on: because the structure is provided by intermediaries who have an interest in opacity. Banks, brokers, trading houses — they profit from the gaps between what buyers know and what sellers know. The information asymmetry is the business model.

AI is a way to close that asymmetry. Not to eliminate intermediaries — some will always be needed — but to make the structure transparent enough that the person who understands the physical world can operate without needing a bank to explain the spread.

## The founder's chair

I didn't know what I didn't know about running a company before I started. The trading floor gives you a very specific set of skills: risk management, commercial instinct, the ability to read a counterpart.

It does not teach you how to build a team, how to raise equity, how to manage a board, how to fire someone who you like personally but who isn't doing the job.

The founder's chair is a different piece of furniture. I'm still learning how to sit in it.
