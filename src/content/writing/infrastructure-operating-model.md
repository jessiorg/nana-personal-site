The three-block model — EMM, Invisible Intelligence, Innovation Hub — is not a corporate structure. It's an operating model.

The insight underneath it is one I've come back to repeatedly: **the data centre floor is the margin**.

## The anchor tenant problem

Every data centre operator has the same problem. Their anchor tenant — the hyperscale cloud provider, the major enterprise — negotiates aggressively on price because they have alternatives. The operator builds capex-heavy infrastructure on the basis of that anchor tenant's commitments, then spends the next decade trying to fill the remaining capacity.

We invert it.

The trading desk is the anchor tenant. It has committed load because it has a genuine, ongoing, physical need for compute. The cost of that compute is directly funded by the revenue the trading desk generates. We built the floor, so we control the economics of the floor.

Then we add external customers on top. They improve utilisation. They reduce the overhead fraction. They make the model more efficient — not by reducing the price we charge each other internally, but by reducing the total cost per unit across the whole system.

## Why own rather than rent

The argument for renting compute is straightforward: flexibility, no capex, no operational overhead.

The argument for owning is less visible but more durable. When you rent, your cost is someone else's revenue model. When you own, your cost is your own inefficiency. You have an incentive to build it efficiently, run it cleanly, and size it correctly.

> The MFU — machine flops_utilised — is our problem to solve, not a vendor's problem that penalises us.

The trading desk's compute needs are predictable enough to justify the capex. The external customers add utilisation on top of a base load we were already carrying.

## The flywheel

Own infrastructure → power the AI → trade on the edge.

Each block makes the others more defensible. The infrastructure makes the AI cheaper to run. The AI makes the trading more profitable. The trading generates the revenue that justifies the infrastructure investment.

That flywheel is what we're building toward over the next 18 months.
